Key Points

Key Concepts — Quick Reference

Speaking Key Concepts

  • OREO IntroductionThe core template: Opinion → Reason → Example → Opinion Restated. Taught in Week 1, used every week after.
  • Making Examples SpecificTurning vague examples ("my friend") into concrete ones ("my friend Rina chose a local college because her family could not afford the fees").
  • Hedging LanguageSoftening claims to sound more academic. "Online learning is better" → "Online learning tends to be more effective in some cases."
  • Responding to Counter-ArgumentsHandling "But what about…?" without losing your position. Staying calm and logical under challenge.
  • Paraphrasing the QuestionRestating the question in your own words before answering. Buys thinking time and shows comprehension.
  • SignpostingUsing discourse markers ("To begin with…", "Moving on to…", "To wrap up…") so the listener can follow your logic.

Writing Key Concepts

  • Spoken → Written ConversionTransforming a spoken OREO response into a written paragraph. Adding cohesive devices (Firstly, For instance, Consequently).
  • Topic SentencesWriting a clear first sentence that announces what the paragraph is about.
  • Concession Structure"While I understand that X, I would argue that Y." Acknowledging the other side before stating your position.
  • Explaining Your EvidenceAdding one sentence after an example to say what it shows, so the example works as evidence rather than just a story.
  • Peer ReviewGiving specific feedback on a partner's writing using structured phrases — not just "good job."
  • Full Essay OverviewSeeing how Introduction + Body 1 + Body 2 + Conclusion connect into one complete piece.
