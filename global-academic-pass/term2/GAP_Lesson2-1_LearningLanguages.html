1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

  • Use the OREO Logic Template (Opinion, Reason, Example, Opinion Restated) to structure spoken responses.
  • Compare how English and other languages are part of daily life in different countries with a partner.
  • Incorporate Academic Vocabulary into discussions as a first step toward academic-level expression.
~1 min Delivery
"Let me quickly walk you through what we will accomplish today. First, you will learn a four-step structure called OREO — Opinion, Reason, Example, Opinion Restated. If you were here last term, this is your chance to make it sharper. Second, you will use that structure to have real discussions about languages with a partner. And third, we will practise upgrading everyday words into more academic ones. These three things together are the core of what IELTS Speaking Part 3 is asking you to do."
Note
Do not spend more than 60 seconds here. The objectives are on screen — point, read, move on. Save energy for the Key Concepts demo.

2. Warm-up (5 mins)

Quick Choice & Why

Choose one and answer in 30 seconds:

A. "Is there an English word you often use, even when you speak your own language?"

B. "How many languages do you hear in a normal week — at home, at school, or online?"

C. "If you could speak one more language perfectly, which would you choose? Why?"

5 mins Setup
"Let's start with a quick warm-up. Look at the three questions on the screen. Choose the one that feels most natural for you, and take about 30 seconds to answer. I will call on each of you. There is no right or wrong answer here — just speak."
Calling on students
"[Student name], which question did you choose? Go ahead." — After each response: "Thank you. Anyone who chose the same question — did you have a different answer?"
Aim to hear from every student at least briefly. If a student gives a very short answer, gently prompt: "Can you tell us a bit more about why?"
Question B often shows a clear difference between countries — some students hear three or more languages every week, others mostly one. If that comes up, point it out briefly: it is useful material for Session 1.
Transition
"Good. Notice that some of you gave a clear reason, and some of you gave mainly a feeling. Today we are going to work on turning those feelings into structured, logical arguments. Let's look at how."
⚠ Do not let this run over 5 minutes. If students are chatty, acknowledge and redirect: "Great energy — let's save that for the discussion later."

3. Key Concepts: The OREO Template (10 mins)

In IELTS Speaking Part 3, examiners assess your ability to develop and justify ideas. Use this structure to organize your response clearly:

Opinion (O)

I believe that... / In my view...

Reason (R)

This is because... / The main reason is...

Example (E)

For instance... / In my country, for example...

Opinion Restated (O)

Therefore... / That is why I think...

Demo

Instructor & Assistant: "Weak Answer" vs. "OREO Answer"

"On YouTube, I guess. It's good."

→ How can we rebuild this with OREO?

Note: The OREO reference sheet has been shared with you — keep it handy.
10 mins Explaining OREO
"In IELTS Speaking Part 3, the examiner wants to see that you can think, not just talk. The OREO template gives you a four-step structure to do exactly that. O is your Opinion — what you believe. R is your Reason — why you believe it. E is your Example — a real situation that supports your reason. And the second O is your Opinion Restated — one sentence that closes your argument by returning to your opinion in a fresh way."
Demo — Weak Answer first
"Watch this. [Assistant], ask me: 'Where do you see or hear English outside class?'"
Assistant asks. You reply: "On YouTube, I guess. It's good." — Then pause and look at students. "So — what is missing from that answer?"
Let students respond. Accept answers like "reason," "example," "details." Then move to the next slide for the Model Response.

3. Key Concepts: The OREO Template (10 mins)

In IELTS Speaking Part 3, examiners assess your ability to develop and justify ideas. Use this structure to organize your response clearly:

Opinion (O)

I believe that... / In my view...

This is because... / The main reason is...

For instance... / In my country, for example...

Opinion Restated (O)

Therefore... / That is why I think...

Model Response

OIn my view, English songs help my English more than anything else I hear outside class.
RThe main reason is that I listen to the same songs again and again, so the phrases stay in my head without any effort.
EFor instance, I learned the phrase "figure it out" from a song I played every morning on the way to school, and a week later I used it naturally when I was talking to a friend.
OThat is why I believe songs are not just entertainment for me — they are one of my most effective English teachers.
Delivering the Model Response
"Now let me try again using OREO." — Read each block naturally as a connected speech, not bullet by bullet. Then point to the color-coded steps on screen: "Notice — yellow is Opinion, blue is Reason, green is Example, purple is Opinion Restated. Four steps, one complete answer."
Point out the last sentence: it returns to the opinion, but it does not copy the first sentence word for word. That is the goal for returning students today.
Closing
"Your OREO sheet is in the chat — please keep it open. You will use it in both discussion sessions today."
⚠ Do not ask students to practise OREO here. The practice happens in Sessions 1 and 2. Keep this to explanation and demo only.

4. Academic Vocabulary

Small changes make a big difference in how academic you sound:

Instead of "good"

  • Practical — "...is practical because..." "Learning phrases from games is practical because you can use them right away."
  • Engaging — "an engaging way to..." "Watching dramas with English subtitles is an engaging way to learn."
  • Effective — "...is effective for..." "Reading aloud is effective for remembering new words."

Instead of "bad"

  • Limited — "...is limited." "Outside school, my chance to speak English is limited."
  • Unnatural — "...sounds unnatural." "Some textbook dialogues sound unnatural in real conversation."
  • Inefficient — "...is inefficient." "Memorising long word lists without ever using them is inefficient."

Instead of "I think":  "In my experience..."  /  "I'm convinced that..."

~4 mins Introduction
"Before we go into the discussions, let's look at one more tool: Academic Vocabulary. The words you choose signal to a listener — a classmate, a teacher, or an examiner — how sophisticated your thinking is. These are not complicated words. They are simply more precise alternatives to words you already use every day. If you were here last term, two of these — effective and inefficient — will be familiar, so they are a quick refresher. The other four are new, and they fit today's topic of learning languages."
Walking through the vocabulary
"Instead of 'good,' try 'practical' — learning phrases from games is practical because you can use them right away. Or 'engaging' — watching dramas with subtitles is an engaging way to learn. Instead of 'bad,' try 'limited' — outside school, my chance to speak English is limited. Or 'unnatural.' And instead of 'I think,' try 'In my experience' or 'I'm convinced that.'"
Read two or three examples aloud — you do not need to cover all of them. Students will pick up the rest through use in Sessions 1 and 2.
Transition into Session 1
"In Session 2, your challenge is to use at least two of these words in your OREO response. Do not force them — let them come naturally. Alright, let's move into the first discussion."

5. 1-on-1 Exchange — Session 1 (15 mins)

PROMPT

"Where do you see or hear English outside your English class? Which of these helps your English the most?"

Steps

  • A: Share opinion using OREO
  • B: Ask one follow-up question
  • A: Respond → "How about you?"
  • B: Share opinion using OREO
  • A: Ask one follow-up question
  • Open discussion — agree, disagree, cultural differences
  • Tell your partner one thing they did well

Follow-up question ideas

  • "How often do you do that?"
  • "Can you give a specific example?"
  • "Is it the same for most students in your country?"
  • "When did you start doing that?"
Today's focus: Answer in all four steps, in order — Opinion, Reason, Example, then Opinion Restated.
When finished: Return to main room — instructor picks 1-2 expressions to highlight (1-2 mins)
15 mins Setup
"Okay, let's go into breakout rooms. You will discuss today's first prompt — it is on the screen: where you see or hear English outside class, and which of those helps your English the most. Person A starts by sharing their opinion using OREO. Person B listens, then asks one follow-up question. After A responds, A turns it around with 'How about you?' — and then B goes through their OREO. After both of you have shared, use the remaining time to discuss freely. Finally, before you come back, tell your partner one specific thing they did well. I will be moving between rooms. Any questions before we start?"
Pairing
Pair students from different countries together where possible — how much English people hear outside class differs a lot between countries, and the contrast is part of the value. If you have both returning students and new joiners, pair them together and let the returning student be Person A, so the new student hears one complete OREO answer before trying it. If numbers are uneven, the Assistant or Instructor steps in to keep all pairs 1-on-1.
During breakout — what to listen for
✓ Is OREO being used, or are students just chatting?
✓ Note 1-2 strong expressions or phrases you hear — you will highlight these after.
⚠ If a pair is silent or stuck, prompt: "Try starting with 'In my view...' and finish that sentence first."
⚠ If a student says they never hear English outside class, redirect: "What about songs, games, signs, or menus? One place is enough."
After Session 1 — feedback (1-2 mins)
"Welcome back. I heard some really good things in the rooms. I want to highlight this expression: [quote what you heard]. Notice how that follows the OREO structure clearly. Well done. Let's keep that energy going in Session 2."
One or two observations maximum. Do not correct errors at this point — save detailed feedback for after Session 2.

6. 1-on-1 Exchange — Session 2 (15 mins)

PROMPT

"When you speak English, what matters more — speaking without stopping, or speaking without mistakes? Why?"

Steps — same format as Session 1

  • A: Share opinion using OREO
  • B: Ask one follow-up question
  • A: Respond → "How about you?"
  • B: Share opinion using OREO
  • A: Ask one follow-up question
  • Open discussion — agree, disagree, cultural differences
  • Tell your partner one thing they did well

Session 2 Challenge

Use at least two of these words in your OREO response:

Practical — "...is practical because..."
Engaging — "an engaging way to..."
Effective — "...is effective for..."
Limited — "...is limited."
Unnatural — "...sounds unnatural."
In my experience... — instead of "I think"
Today's focus: Close with your Opinion Restated — one fresh sentence that returns to your opinion, not a copy of your first.
When finished: Return to main room — instructor feedback & group discussion (3 mins)
15 mins Setup
"Same format as before, same steps — but a new prompt, and one extra challenge. This time, try to use at least two of the Academic Vocabulary words listed on the right side of the screen. The goal is not to force them in — it is to find a natural moment where a more precise word fits. Ready? Into breakout rooms."
During breakout — what to listen for
✓ Are students attempting the Academic Vocabulary? Even one word used naturally is worth highlighting.
✓ Is the closing Opinion Restated a fresh sentence, or a copy of the first one? Note one good example to share.
⚠ If a student is struggling with the vocabulary, do not intervene immediately. Let them work through it and note it for the group feedback.
After Session 2 — group feedback (3 mins)
"Welcome back. Good work. Let me share a couple of things I noticed. First, [positive observation — e.g., 'I heard someone use "practical" very naturally in their Reason step — that is exactly what we are aiming for.']. Second, one area to work on: [one common issue — e.g., 'A few of you gave a strong Opinion and Reason, but the closing Opinion Restated was quite short. Next time, try closing with one full sentence that echoes your Opinion in a fresh way.']."
Experience corner — asking the Assistant / Instructor
"Assistant / Instructor — when you were learning English, was there a moment when you stopped worrying about mistakes and just spoke? What changed?"
Keep it to one short story. If there was no single moment, ask what helped most over time — the question works either way, and it speaks directly to today's Session 2 prompt.

7. Reflection (8 mins)

Task 1 — Breakout Rooms (5 mins)

Room 1 Japanese students  |  Room 2 Indonesian students

In your own language, discuss:

"Which step of OREO was the hardest for you today — and why?"

Task 2 — Main Room (3 mins)

Share one reflection with everyone:

  • "Today I found it difficult to..."
  • "One expression I want to use more is..."
  • "Something my partner said that surprised me was..."
8 mins Setup
"Alright, last activity. We are going to split into two rooms — Japanese students in Room 1, Indonesian students in Room 2. In your room, discuss in your own language: which step of OREO felt hardest today, and why? The Assistant will join Room 1 and the Instructor will join Room 2 to give advice based on what they hear. You have five minutes. Then we come back together for a quick final share."
Brief for the Assistant / Instructor (share before class)
Ask students the question, listen, and offer one piece of practical advice each. For example: "If Example is hard, think of a moment from your own life first, then put it into English." One or two sentences of genuine advice per student is enough.
Task 2 — Main room (3 mins)
"Welcome back. Can one person from each group share — what was the hardest step today, and what advice did you get?"
Hear from 2-3 students. Do not go around the whole group — time is short.

Logic Map for Next Week

Next week's class is Logic & Writing — we will turn a spoken argument into a written paragraph. To prepare, fill in the Logic Map Reference Sheet (shared with you as a PDF) for the prompt below.

PROMPT

"It is better for children to begin learning a foreign language at primary school than at secondary school. To what extent do you agree or disagree?"

WHAT TO DO (required)
Complete the four parts of the map: Thesis, one Reason, one Example, and one Counter-argument with your response. Keep each line short and specific — that is more useful than long, vague sentences.
IF POSSIBLE (optional)
If you have time, turn your Logic Map into a short paragraph (about 80–100 words) using OREO. This step is optional — next week's class works well either way.

  Bring your completed map to next week's class — and your short paragraph too, if you wrote one. We will build on whichever you bring.

~1 min Delivery
"One last thing before we finish — your homework for next week. Next class is Logic & Writing, where we will take the thinking we practised today and turn it into written academic paragraphs. To prepare, I have shared a Logic Map Reference Sheet with you as a PDF. On page 2 of that sheet, fill in a Logic Map for the prompt on screen — whether it is better for children to begin learning a foreign language at primary school than at secondary school. Just complete the four parts of the map: Thesis, Reason, Example, and one Counter-argument with your response — that part is required. If you have extra time, you can also turn your map into a short paragraph, about 80 to 100 words, using OREO — but that part is optional. Bring whatever you have to class and we will build on it together."
About the question form
This term, every Writing prompt ends with "To what extent do you agree or disagree?" — the way many IELTS Writing Task 2 questions end. If students ask, tell them nothing new is needed: the Thesis line simply says how far they agree, and why.
Why the optional paragraph was added
Feedback from a previous run showed many students could not finish drafting during Writing class time. Asking students to attempt a short paragraph at home — optionally — means Writing class can focus on revising with a partner's feedback instead of drafting from zero. The Logic Map alone remains fully sufficient; the paragraph is a bonus for students who have time.
If students look uncertain
Point them to the worked example on page 1 of the Logic Map Reference Sheet (the smartphone-ban sample). Remind them: short, specific lines are better than long vague ones. Four blanks, not four paragraphs — the paragraph is only for those with extra time.
Closing
"That is it for today. Thank you — see you next week for Logic & Writing."
